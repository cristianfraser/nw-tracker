# US federal tax readings for the demo's Form 1040 (wave R2, research step)

Date: 2026-11-10. Taxpayer profile: the fictional demo person — a Chilean citizen who became a US tax
resident on 2024-04-15 (single, W-2 employee in Texas), with two rented apartments in Chile with
mortgages (one a former home converted to rental in 2024-06), a Chilean money-market mutual fund (held
since 2020), a Chilean-listed equity ETF (bought 2025), US stocks at a Chilean broker (W-8BEN, 15 %
withheld) and at a US broker, bitcoin at a Chilean exchange, the Chilean mandatory pension (AFP) and
voluntary pension savings (APV) in mutual funds. Tax years 2024 and 2025.

Method: primary sources only, fetched and read on 2026-11-10 — IRS Publications 514, 519, 527, 550, 946;
Form 1040 / schedule / form instructions and the blank forms (2024 and 2025); Rev. Procs. 2023-34 and
2024-40; Notices 2023-55 and 2023-80; the Code (law.cornell.edu) and Treasury regulations (law.cornell.edu
/cfr); the Chile–US convention text, Treasury's Technical Explanation (Treasury "TE") and the 2023
exchange of notes (irs.gov/pub/irs-trty); Treasury's entry-into-force announcement. Quotes are under 15
words. The 2025 editions of publications are the current ones; the 2024 editions were not read
separately (the rules cited are statutory/regulatory; differences are noted). «UNVERIFIED» marks what no
primary source I could reach settles, with what I tried.

## 1. Dual-status year 2024 (Pub. 519)

**Residency start.** Pub. 519 ch. 1 «Residency starting date under substantial presence test»: «residency
starting date is generally the first day you are present» in the US; up to 10 days may be excluded with a
statement showing a closer connection and a tax home abroad. Arriving 2024-04-15 and staying gives 261 days
in 2024, so he meets the test for 2024 on its own; the first-year choice (§7701(b)(4)) is only for a person
who meets neither test for the year, so it is irrelevant. Confidence: high.

**Income by part** (Pub. 519 ch. 6 «Income Subject to Tax»). Resident part: «taxed on income from all
sources»; the income is taxable «even if you earned it while you were a nonresident alien» if received as a
resident. Nonresident part: «taxed on income from U.S. sources» (and some effectively connected foreign
income); foreign-source income not effectively connected «is not taxable if you receive it while a
nonresident», even if earned while resident. So the RECEIPT date splits foreign-source income (a Chilean
rent received 2024-04-10 is out, 2024-04-20 in), and foreign gains realised before 2024-04-15 are out. US
-source FDAP of the NR part (US dividends before the move) is taxed at «the flat 30% rate or lower treaty
rate. You cannot take any deductions against this income» (ch. 6 «How To Figure Your Tax»). Confidence: high.
**Plan refinement:** the NR statement is not empty — it lists the NR-part US dividends (Chilean broker, IBKR).

**Standard deduction — confirmed gone.** Ch. 6 «Restrictions for Dual-Status Taxpayers»: «You cannot use
the standard deduction allowed on Form 1040 or 1040-SR. However, you can itemize any allowable deductions.»
Form mechanics: 2024 Form 1040 has one box «Spouse itemizes on a separate return or you were a dual-status
alien» (instructions: check it, standard deduction zero); 2025 splits it — 12b spouse itemizes, **12c «You
were a dual-status alien»** (2025 instructions: «If you checked the box on line 12c, your standard
deduction is zero»). Remaining deductions: Schedule A only. Texas has no income tax; §164(b)(6)(A) «foreign
real property taxes shall not be taken into account» as an itemized deduction (rental property taxes stay
deductible on Schedule E — Q4); his mortgage interest is rental interest (Schedule E). Adjustments on
Schedule 1 are unaffected. **No QBI deduction:** §199A(c)(3)(A)(i) takes only items «effectively connected
with the conduct of a trade or business within the United States» — Chilean rentals never qualify (13a = 0).
Confidence: high.

**Forms.** Resident at year end: «You must file Form 1040 or 1040-SR … Check the "Other" checkbox … write
"Dual Status Return"»; «Attach a statement … to show the income for the part of the year you are a
nonresident. You can use Form 1040-NR as the statement» marked «Dual Status Stmt» (Pub. 519 ch. 6 «Forms To
File — Resident at end of year»). Dual-status returns cannot be e-filed. Confidence: high.

**Elections.** Whole-year resident choice (§6013(g)/(h)): needs a spouse — Pub. 519 ch. 1 «Choosing Resident
Alien Status»: «If you are single at the end of the year, you cannot make this choice.» Confidence: high.

**Rates and restrictions.** Ch. 6: no head-of-household column or worksheet; no joint return; the married
-filing-separately column rule concerns married NRs only. An unmarried dual-status filer uses the ordinary
**Single** Tax Table / Tax Computation Worksheet; credits follow resident rules except EIC/education credits.
Single brackets: 2024 (Rev. Proc. 2023-34 table 3) 10 % to 11 600, 12 % to 47 150, 22 % to 100 525, 24 % to
191 950, 32 % to 243 725, 35 % to 609 350, 37 % above (the Rev. Proc. prints «excess over $191,150» for the 32 %
base — a typo; the edge is 191 950); 2025 (Rev. Proc. 2024-40 table 3) 11 925 / 48 475 / 103 350 / 197 300 /
250 525 / 626 350. Confidence: high.

**What this changes in the plan.** Agrees (no std deduction, no election). Adds: NR statement carries US
FDAP; receipt dates split foreign income; 13a = 0; 2025 is a full-resident year (box 12c unchecked).

## 2. US dividends through a Chilean broker (W-8BEN, 15 % kept)

**Where claimed.** Instructions for Form 1040 line 25c (2024 and 2025): «Also include on line 25c any tax
withheld that is shown on Form 1042-S, Form 8805, or Form 8288-A» (2025: «attach the form to your return»).
**Line 25c («Other forms»), not 25b** (25b = «Form(s) 1099»). Review item 1 says 25b — wrong. Confidence: high.

**Not a foreign tax.** Form 1116/Pub. 514 are for foreign income taxes (§901); tax withheld by the US
withholding agent is a US payment, never an FTC input. Treaty dates: Treasury's announcement («Treasury
Announces Entry into Force of Income Tax Treaty with Chile») — withholding taxes «for amounts paid or credited
on or after February 1, 2024»; «for all other taxes … taxable periods beginning on or after January 1,
2024» (Pub. 901 agrees). So US dividends paid before 2024-02-01 were subject to 30 % (no treaty), after that
15 % (Art. 10(2)(b)). As a resident the holder should file W-9; the 15 % kept after the move is a story
choice and is simply credited on 25c against the tax on the same dividends. Confidence: high on the line,
medium on rates by date (broker practice not audited).

## 3. Basis on becoming a resident; currency translation

**No step-up.** No rule in the Code or Pub. 519/550 revalues an alien's assets on becoming resident; basis
stays cost (§1012). Direct evidence: the PFIC regulation's own example — a NR who bought for $50 «must use
its original basis of $50» for §1001 after becoming a US person (Reg. §1.1296-1(d)(5)(ii) Example). So
stocks and crypto keep their historic USD cost (acquisition-date rate). Confidence: high (negative rule).

**Translation.** Pub. 514 «Foreign Currency and Exchange Rates» / IRS page «Foreign currency …»: «Use the
exchange rate prevailing when you receive, pay, or accrue the item»; «If there is more than one exchange
rate, use the one that most properly reflects your income.» Basis = pesos paid at the purchase-date rate;
proceeds at the sale-date rate. IRS «Yearly average currency exchange rates»: «The Internal Revenue Service
has no official exchange rate. Generally, it accepts any posted exchange rate that is used consistently.» So
the stored daily spot (`fx_daily`) is acceptable everywhere; a yearly average is an accepted alternative for
recurring income such as rents if used consistently (spot per receipt is the strict reading; Reg.
§1.988-1(d)(3) allows a quarterly-or-shorter convention for receivables/payables). Confidence: high.

**§988.** Disposing of pesos acquired earlier is a §988 transaction; §988(e)(2): no gain for a «personal
transaction» «if the gain … exceeds $200» it is recognised; §988(e)(3) excludes transactions whose expenses
fall under §162/§212 — so pesos used for investments or for the rental mortgage are not personal: ordinary
exchange gain/loss. Not modelled (the plan says so; add the UF mortgage repayment and the converted rent
pesos). Confidence: medium (statute read, no ruling).

## 4. Schedule E for Chilean rentals

**ADS required.** §168(g)(1)(A): «any tangible property which during the taxable year is used predominantly
outside the United States». Pub. 527 ch. 2: «You must use GDS unless you are specifically required by law to
use ADS»; Form 4562 Part III Section C. Confidence: high.

**Period.** §168(g)(2)(C)(iii) (as amended by P.L. 115-97): residential rental property **30 years**; Pub. 527
Table 2-1: GDS 27.5 / ADS «30 years», footnote «40 years for property placed in service before January 1,
2018». Straight line, mid-month. Both apartments (rental since 2020; converted 2024) are 30 years. Land not
depreciable: «You can't depreciate the cost of land». Confidence: high.

**Former residence converted (Vitacura).** Reg. §1.168(i)-4(b)(1): the allowance is «determined as though
the property is placed in service … on the date on which the conversion occurs»; Pub. 527: basis for
depreciation is «the lesser of its adjusted basis or its FMV» at conversion (building only; the regulation's
example uses FMV «excluding land»). Confidence: high.

**Centro (rental since 2020, before he became a US person): the plan's «allowed or allowable from
placed-in-service» is UNVERIFIED.** Searched: §1016(a)(2); Reg. §1.1016-3(a) («allowed … but not less than the
amount allowable»; straight line if none claimed); Reg. §1.168(i)-4 (conversion; includes property «previously
used by a tax-exempt entity before the entity changed to a taxable entity» as placed in service at the
conversion date at lesser of FMV/adjusted basis — the closest analogy for property entering the tax base);
Pubs. 519, 527, 946. No authority addresses a foreign rental of a new US resident. Two readings: **(A)** keep the
2020 placed-in-service date; allowable depreciation of the NR years (≈ 3,9 years at 30-year ADS) reduces basis;
**(B)** fresh placing in service 2024-04-15 at the lesser of adjusted basis and FMV. The plan chose (A);
implement behind one constant and state the reading on Schedule E. Confidence: low.

**Expenses.** Pub. 527 ch. 1 lists mortgage interest, insurance, management fees, repairs; deductible from
rental income. Foreign real property taxes on rental property: §164(b)(6)(A) disallows them as an itemized
deduction, but the rule does not apply to taxes «paid or accrued in carrying on a trade or business or an
activity described in section 212» (§164(a) last sentence; §164(b)(6) closing sentence) — contribuciones
on the rentals deduct on Schedule E. Gastos comunes, the agency's 7 %, insurance deduct. Schedule E line 1a for
a foreign property: «enter the city, province or state, country, and postal code». Pesos per Q3. Not
modelled: exchange gain/loss on the UF-indexed loan (§988). Confidence: high.

## 5. Passive activity losses (Form 8582, §469)

§469(i)(1)–(2): the offset covers rental real estate activities in which the individual «actively
participated», max «$25,000»; (i)(3)(A): reduced by «50 percent of the amount by which the adjusted gross
income … exceeds $100,000» → zero at 150 000 (AGI without passive losses, taxable social security etc.,
(i)(3)(E); Pub. 527 ch. 3: «$25,000 for single individuals»; «limited to 50% of the difference between
$150,000 and your MAGI»). **Foreign rentals are not excluded** — neither §469(i) nor Pub. 527 carves them out;
active participation = ≥ 10 % ownership plus management decisions (approving tenants/terms/expenditures —
an agency does not defeat it). He is above 150 000 → allowance zero; disallowed losses are suspended
(§469(b): «treated as a deduction allocable to such activity in the next year») and released on a full
taxable disposition (§469(g)). Confidence: high.

## 6. Foreign tax credit (Form 1116)

**Accrued election.** Pub. 514 «Choosing to take credit in the year taxes accrue»: check «Accrued» on a
timely original return; «You cannot make this choice on an amended return. Once you make that choice, you
must follow it in all later years.» Binding; applies to all foreign taxes. Accrued taxes translate at the
average rate of the year to which they relate when paid within 24 months, not later, and the currency is not
inflationary; otherwise payment-date rate (Pub. 514 «Exception»). Confidence: high.

**Category / limit / carry.** Passive category income includes «Rents.», interest, dividends, annuities, net
gain on property that generates passive income (Pub. 514 «Separate Limit Income»; active business rents
excepted). Limit: credit ≤ «total U.S. tax liability multiplied by a fraction» of foreign taxable income in
the category over total taxable income; a category with zero or negative foreign taxable income has limit
zero. Excess: carry back one year, forward 10 («carry them forward to the 10 years following», Pub. 514
«Carrybacks and carryovers»; §904(c)). Confidence: high.

**Source matters (new).** §865(a)(1): income from the sale of personal property «by a United States
resident shall be sourced in the United States»: gains on the ETF, the fund and bitcoin are US-source, so a
Chilean tax on a gain has no foreign income to credit against (limit zero) unless re-sourced; §1296(c)(2)
sources MTM inclusions like a sale → US. Rents are foreign-source. Chilean fund distributions are foreign
passive dividends.

**Is the 35 % Impuesto Adicional on gross rent creditable?**
1. *Treaty + regulation (the plan missed it).* Art. 23(1) (as restated by the 2023 exchange of notes, in force
   with the Convention on 2023-12-19): «the taxes referred to in subparagraph b) of paragraph 3 and paragraph
   4 of Article 2 … shall be considered income taxes»; Art. 2(3)(b): «the taxes imposed under the Income Tax
   Act (Ley sobre Impuesto a la Renta)». Reg. §1.901-2(a)(1)(iii): «A foreign levy that is treated as an income
   tax under the relief from double taxation article of an income tax treaty … is a foreign income tax if the
   levy is … paid by a citizen or resident of the United States that elects benefits under the treaty.» TE Art. 23
   ¶1: credits are «subject to the limitations of U.S. law» (§904 limit, §986 translation, US carryovers, the
   specific-economic-benefit rule). Disclosure: Reg. §301.6114-1(a)(3) Example 2 treats a tax listed as
   creditable in a treaty with no other authority as a position to disclose → probably **Form 8833**
   (UNVERIFIED whether the IRS requires it here).
2. *Domestic law:* a gross-basis tax with no deductions fails the 2022 final cost-recovery rule.
3. *Relief:* Notice 2023-55 §3 lets taxpayers apply former §1.901-2(a)/(b) with: «No foreign tax whose base is
   gross receipts or gross income satisfies the net income requirement, except … a foreign tax whose base
   consists solely of investment income that is not derived from a trade or business, or wage income (or
   both).» Notice 2023-80 §5.04 redefines the relief period as «taxable years beginning on or after December
   28, 2021, and ending before the date that a notice or other guidance withdrawing or modifying the
   temporary relief is issued» — **covers 2024 and 2025; no withdrawal found** (Pub. 514 (2025): extended «until
   further notice»; IRS 2025–26 priority guidance lists future §901/903 regulations). Rent as «investment
   income not derived from a trade or business» is plausible for a passive landlord, not certain. Using the relief is
   all-or-nothing for the year's foreign taxes.
Verdict: creditable under (1) regardless of (2)/(3), subject to §904. Confidence: medium-high.

**10 % withholdings.** The treaty caps dividends at 15 % (Art. 10(2)(b)) and gives the residence State
exclusive taxation of gains on shares sold on a recognised exchange (Art. 13(6)(c)) and of «property other than…»
(Art. 13(8): «taxable only in the Contracting State of which the alienator is a resident»). A Chilean 10 %
único on the ETF sale by a US resident therefore exceeds the treaty: not a compulsory payment (Reg.
§1.901-2(e)(5)) and, being US-source gain, not creditable anyway. **Plan change:** do not credit it; state it.

## 7. PFICs (Form 8621)

**Marketable stock, open-end fund.** Reg. §1.1296-2(a): PFIC stock «regularly traded … on a qualified exchange»
or «Stock in certain PFICs, as described in paragraph (d)». (d)(1): the foreign corporation offers/has stock
«redeemable at its net asset value» and, at all times in the year: (i) more than 100 shareholders in the class,
(ii) available to the public at NAV with no minimum above US$10 000, (iii) quotations published at least weekly
in a medium not controlled by the issuer, (iv) annual audited public financials, (v) supervised as an
investment company, (vi) «no senior securities», (vii) ≥ 90 % passive income, (viii) ≥ 90 % passive assets;
(d)(2) anti-abuse. A CMF-supervised Chilean fondo mutuo plausibly meets (ii)–(v), (vii)–(viii); (i) and (vi)
need checking; and whether a fondo (patrimony without legal personality) is a «foreign corporation» is an
entity-classification question the app should not decide — **UNVERIFIED** (tried the regulation and Form 8621
instructions). If not marketable: default §1291, flag only.

**Listed ETF.** (c)(1)(ii): a foreign exchange «regulated or supervised by a governmental authority» with
volume/listing/disclosure/surveillance rules «designed to prevent fraudulent and manipulative acts» and rules
that «effectively promote active trading». Regularly traded: «other than in de minimis quantities, on at
least 15 days during each calendar quarter» ((b)(1); IPO-year rule (b)(2)). No IRS list; the Bolsa de
Santiago is a reasonable candidate — **UNVERIFIED**; same classification caveat.

**Basis on becoming a US person.** Reg. §1.1296-1(d)(5)(i): «If any individual becomes a United States person
in a taxable year beginning after December 31, 1997, solely for purposes of this section, the adjusted basis …
of any section 1296 stock owned by such individual on the first day of such taxable year shall be treated as
being the greater of its fair market value or its adjusted basis on such first day.» Corrections to the plan:
(a) GREATER of FMV and adjusted basis (no loss recognised on a position held below cost); (b) the text says
«first day of such taxable year» (the example uses Jan. 1) — literally 2024-01-01, not the arrival date;
for a mid-year arrival this is **UNVERIFIED** (no ruling found); recommend FMV at 2024-04-15 behind a constant.
Election: (h)(1)(i): made «on the Form 8621 … included with the original tax return» for the first year the stock is held
at year end, due date incl. extensions; continues until revoked/terminated ((h)(2),(3)); late only under
§301.9100 ((h)(1)(iii)). Fund: 2024; ETF: 2025.

**First-year coordination (Reg. §1.1296-1(i), Form 8621 Part V).** If the corporation was a PFIC in any prior
year «during the United States person's holding period» without QEF treatment, the first MTM year applies §1291
to the year-end excess instead of Part IV. Whether years held as a nonresident count is not settled in what I
could reach — **UNVERIFIED**; this is the largest open risk for the fund (held since 2020). Implement Part IV,
flag Part V as «possible».

**Inclusion, loss limit, sale.** §1296(a): year-end FMV above adjusted basis is ordinary income (§1296(c)(1));
a loss is allowed «only to the extent of» unreversed inclusions; basis rises by inclusions, falls by
deductions (§1296(b)). Unreversed inclusions = prior inclusions − prior deductions (Reg. §1.1296-1(a)(3)). Sale:
gain ordinary; loss ordinary only up to unreversed inclusions, the excess under general loss rules (Reg.
§1.1296-1(c)(4)); Form 8621 Part IV lines 13–14c. For §1001 the original basis stays cost plus MTM adjustments
(the reg's example), the part of a gain above the ordinary part is long-term if held over a year.

**Where.** Form 8621 instructions Part IV: «include the gain or (loss) on the "other income" line» → **Schedule
1 line 8z** (both years). §1291 tax: 2025 line 16 instructions, «Any tax from Form 8621, line 16e … Check box 3».
**Distributions** from an MTM-elected PFIC are ordinary dividends, **never qualified**: Pub. 550: «A corporation
is not a qualified foreign corporation if it is a passive foreign investment company during its tax year … or
… previous tax year» → 3b only (Schedule B).

**Reporting threshold.** Reg. §1.1298-1(c)(2): no Form 8621 «with respect to a section 1291 fund» if aggregate PFIC
stock is ≤ $25,000 (≤ $50,000 joint) on the last day, no excess distribution/gain, no QEF election. **Only §1291
funds**; an MTM-elected holding still files Parts I and IV (Form 8621 instructions: a filer with an MTM election
«will file a single Form 8621 and complete Part I and Part IV»); for measuring the $25 000 all PFIC stock counts,
MTM included. Other exceptions: (c)(3) stock marked to market under another provision; (c)(4) pension funds (Q8).

**Default §1291 in two sentences.** An excess distribution (or gain on sale) is spread ratably over the holding
period; prior years' shares are taxed at the highest rate of each year plus interest, the current-year share as
ordinary income, and gain is never capital (§1291(a)–(c)). Tax and interest are computed on Form 8621 Part III.

## 8. Chilean pensions (treaty Art. 18; Form 8833; PFIC)

**Text.** Art. 18(4): «Where a resident of a Contracting State is a beneficiary of a plan established in the other
Contracting State that is generally exempt from income taxation in that other State and operated to provide
pension or retirement benefits, income earned but not distributed by the plan shall be taxable in either State
only at such time as and, subject to paragraph 1, to the extent that a payment … is made». TE Art. 18 ¶4: «neither
State will tax the income earned but not distributed». Saving clause: Protocol ¶4(a): paragraphs 1(b), 3, 4 and 6
of Art. 18 are excepted; TE: «the United States will not tax U.S. citizens and residents on the income described
in those paragraphs». So a US resident's Chilean plan that is «generally exempt from income taxation» in Chile
defers its undistributed earnings.

**AFP.** Protocol ¶18: for Art. 18(3) the Chilean social security system includes «the social security system
created by Decree Law 3500 (DL 3500)» → payments under it are «taxable only in the first-mentioned State»
(Chile; Art. 18(3)). Art. 18(5)'s contribution relief is for persons «temporarily present» and is limited to
relief the host State gives its own residents; a resident alien employed in the US is not within its purpose;
his pre-move contributions are irrelevant to the 1040. Confidence: medium-high (texts read; no IRS ruling).

**APV in mutual funds.** Art. 18(4) needs a plan «generally exempt from income taxation» in Chile and operated
for pension/retirement benefits. Whether the APV qualifies is a Chilean-law plus IRS-position question the
treaty does not answer; Protocol ¶19's list names DL 3500 plans but only for ¶5. **UNVERIFIED**; tried treaty,
TE, protocol, Reg. §1.1298-1; no IRS guidance on APV found. State the reading (APV treated as Art. 18(4)-covered,
deferral, Form 8833 flag) and name the alternative (each APV fund a PFIC).

**PFIC switch-off.** Reg. §1.1298-1(c)(4): a shareholder who is a beneficiary of «a plan, trust, scheme, or other
arrangement that is treated as a foreign pension fund (or equivalent) under an income tax treaty … that owns,
directly or indirectly, an interest in a PFIC is not required … to file Form 8621 … if, pursuant to the applicable
income tax treaty, the income earned by the foreign pension fund may be taxed as the income of the shareholder only
when and to the extent the income is paid». This switches off **reporting** only, for PFICs held through the
arrangement; that the §1291/MTM taxes do not arise follows from Art. 18(4) itself, not from the regulation.
Confidence: high on the reg text, conclusion for APV unverified.

**Form 8833.** Reg. §301.6114-1(c)(1)(iv) waives reporting for the position «That a treaty reduces or modifies the
taxation of income derived from … pensions, annuities, social security and other public pensions» → a pension
deferral position is probably waived; the Art. 23 credit position (Q6) and residency determined under a treaty
((b)(8)) are the ones that may need Form 8833. Confidence: medium.

**Also.** Art. 4(1) excludes from «resident» a person liable «in respect only of income from sources in that
State» — a Chilean NR taxed on Chilean-source income only is not a Chilean treaty resident, so no tie-breaker (Art.
4(2): permanent home, centre of vital interests, habitual abode, nationality) arises while the F22 treats him as
NR (R1's declaration). If Chile kept him resident, nationality could favour Chile.

## 9. Qualified dividends and capital gains

**Thresholds (single).** 0 % up to taxable income **2024 $47,025**, 15 % up to **$518,900**, 20 % above (Rev. Proc.
2023-34 §3.12); **2025 $48,350 / $533,400** (Rev. Proc. 2024-40 §3.12; the 2025 worksheet lines 6 and 13 print them).
Standard deduction $14,600 (2024); **$15,750 for 2025** — Rev. Proc. 2024-40 prints $15,000 but P.L. 119-21 raised it
(2025 Form 1040 margin «Single or Married filing separately, $15,750»; irrelevant to a dual-status 2024).

**Worksheet outline** (Instructions, «Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet — Line 16»): 1 taxable
income; 2 qualified dividends (3a); 3 smaller of Schedule D lines 15/16 (or 7a); 4 = 2+3; 5 = 1−4 (ordinary part);
6 the 0 % edge; 7–9 the portion of income under that edge and the 0 %-taxed amount (line 9); 10–12 the
remaining preferential income; 13 the 15 % edge; 14–17 the part that fits below it (taxed at 15 %, line 18); 19–21
the rest at 20 %; then tax on line 5 at ordinary rates, sum, and the smaller of that and the ordinary Tax
Table/Computation Worksheet tax → line 16. Test against the IRS example. Confidence: thresholds exact; step
numbers beyond 17 not quoted.

**PFIC dividends never qualified** (Pub. 550, above). **Holding period** for qualified dividends: «more than 60
days during the 121-day period that begins 60 days before the ex-dividend date» (Pub. 550). Long-term: over one
year, holding period «begins on the day after you acquired» (IRS virtual-currency FAQ A6).

**Crypto.** IRS virtual currency FAQs (Notice 2014-21): «treated as property»; basis «the amount you spent to
acquire» it in US dollars (A8); gain = amount received less adjusted basis (A7). Form 8949: 2025 adds digital-asset
boxes G–I (short-term) and J–L (long-term): «Use box G, H, or I to report short-term digital asset transactions. Do
not use box C» (Instructions for Form 8949 (2025)); 2024 uses boxes C/F and the Form 1040 «Digital assets» question.
**Wash sales:** §1091 covers «stock or securities»; the 2025 Schedule D instructions extend wash sale rules to
digital assets only that are also stock or securities («tokenized securities»). Ordinary crypto (bitcoin) is
outside §1091 for 2024–2025 (inference; the IRS has no explicit «wash sales don't apply to crypto» statement).
Wash sales do apply to the stocks/ETFs; the plan's «not modelled» should say stocks only.

## 10. Net investment income tax (Form 8960, §1411)

Threshold: §1411(b)(3) «in any other case, $200,000» (single, not indexed); Form 8960 instructions: «Single or
Head of household is $200,000». 3,8 % of the lesser of NII or MAGI over the threshold. Dual-status: «subject to the
NIIT only for the portion of the year you were a U.S. resident. The relevant threshold amount isn't reduced or
prorated». NII: interest, dividends, rents, royalties, net gains; PFIC: Reg. §1.1411-10 — excess distributions
that are dividends are NII, and «Amounts included in gross income under section 1296(a)(1) and amounts allowed as a
deduction under section 1296(a)(2) are taken into account in determining net gain attributable to the disposition
of property». Rents on Schedule E from a passive rental are NII. **No FTC against NIIT:** Reg. §1.1411-1(e): FTC
«are not allowed as a credit against the section 1411 tax». Wages are not NII but count in MAGI. Confidence: high.
The crossing of US$200 000 is computed, not asserted.

- **Form 8938, single filer living in the US:** «more than $50,000 on the last day of the tax year» or «$75,000 at
  any time» (IRS summary of FATCA reporting). Dual-status NR-months treatment not stated — UNVERIFIED.
- **Form 2210:** penalty may apply if withholding and estimates «didn't equal at least the smaller of: 90% of your
  2025 tax, or 100% of your 2024 tax» (110 % when prior-year AGI exceeds $150,000; the prior year must cover 12
  months). Rents, gains and MTM inclusions are not withheld. Not modelled.

## 12. Options (Pub. 550; §1234; §1256)

- Short option expired: «If your obligation expires, the amount you received … is short-term capital gain»; closed
  by buying back: «short-term capital gain or loss» (Pub. 550 «Writers of puts and calls»; §1234(b)).
- Long option sold: «either a long-term or short-term capital gain or loss, depending on how long you held it»;
  expired: capital loss «depending on your holding period, which ends on the expiration date». Exercised long call:
  add cost to stock basis; long put: reduce amount realized.
- Assigned short put: «decrease your basis in the stock by the amount you received for the put. Your holding period
  for the stock begins on the date you buy it». Assigned short call: «increase your amount realized on the sale of
  the underlying stock by the amount you received for the call». Form 8949: a premium missing from the 1099-B
  proceeds goes in column (g), code E.
- Not §1256: «A section 1256 contract is any: Regulated futures contract, Foreign currency contract, Nonequity
  option, Dealer equity option, …» — equity options of a non-dealer are not.
- Qualified covered calls: Pub. 550 has sections «Qualified covered call options and optioned stock» and «Capital
  loss on qualified covered call options» (straddle rules, §1092(c)); an unqualified covered call is a straddle
  (loss deferral, holding-period suspension). Detail UNVERIFIED (§1092(c)(4) text not retrieved); not modelled.
